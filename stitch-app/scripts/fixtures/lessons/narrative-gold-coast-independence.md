## Why This Matters

Ghana was the first sub-Saharan African colony to win independence, and its path shaped movements across the continent.

## Key Terms to Know First

The **Convention People's Party (CPP)** was founded by Kwame Nkrumah in 1949. **Positive Action** was its 1950 campaign of strikes and non-violent protest.

## Who and When

Kwame Nkrumah returned to the Gold Coast in 1947 to work for the United Gold Coast Convention, then broke away to form the CPP in June 1949.

## Causal Chain

Post-war economic grievances -> the 1948 Accra riots -> the Coussey Committee's constitutional reforms -> the 1951 election won by the CPP -> internal self-government -> independence on 6 March 1957.

## A Sourced Episode

On 28 February 1948, police fired on ex-servicemen marching to petition the governor at Christiansborg Castle. The shootings sparked riots in Accra.

## Competing Accounts

Some historians stress Nkrumah's mass mobilisation; others argue British officials were already planning a gradual handover after 1948.

## What Changed After

Independence made Accra a centre for Pan-African politics, hosting the All-African People's Conference in 1958.

## Word Bank

- **CPP**: the Convention People's Party, founded in 1949
- **Positive Action**: the CPP's 1950 campaign of strikes and protest
- **Self-government**: rule by an elected local government under colonial oversight
