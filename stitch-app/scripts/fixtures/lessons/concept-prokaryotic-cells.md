## Why This Matters

Every living thing is built from cells, but not all cells are organised the same way. Knowing the difference between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells explains why antibiotics can kill bacteria without harming your own cells.

## Key Terms to Know First

A **prokaryote** is a single-celled organism without a nucleus. A **eukaryote** has a nucleus enclosed by a nuclear envelope. The **nucleoid** is the region where a prokaryote keeps its DNA.

## The Idea

Prokaryotic cells lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles; their DNA sits in the nucleoid. Bacteria and archaea are prokaryotes. Eukaryotic cells contain organelles such as mitochondria, the endoplasmic reticulum and the Golgi apparatus.

### Size comparison

| Cell type | Typical diameter |
|---|---|
| Prokaryote | 1-5 um |
| Eukaryote | 10-100 um |

Because a eukaryote is usually far larger, its volume grows faster than its surface area: if r > 10 um, diffusion alone cannot supply the cell, so it relies on internal membranes.

## Examples and Non-Examples

*Escherichia coli* is a prokaryote. A yeast cell is not, even though it is also single-celled, because it has a nucleus.

## The Attractive Wrong Idea

Students often think the nucleoid is a small nucleus. The idea is that the nucleoid has no membrane around it at all, so it is a region, not an organelle.

## Use It

A new organism has DNA floating in the cytoplasm and no mitochondria. Classify it and explain your reasoning in two sentences.

## Recap from Memory

## Word Bank

- **Prokaryote**: a cell without a nucleus or membrane-bound organelles
- **Eukaryote**: a cell with a nucleus enclosed by a nuclear envelope
- **Nucleoid**: the region of a prokaryote that holds its DNA
- **Organelle**: a membrane-bound compartment inside a eukaryotic cell
